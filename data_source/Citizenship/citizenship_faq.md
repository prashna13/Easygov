# Citizenship Certificate FAQ — Nepal

---

## Category 1: Application & Eligibility

**Q: What is the minimum age to apply for a Nepali citizenship certificate?**
A: 16 years old. Once you turn 16, you are legally eligible to apply for citizenship by descent if at least one parent is a Nepali citizen.

**Q: Where do I apply for a citizenship certificate?**
A: The process starts at your local Ward Office (Wada Karyalaya), which issues the Sifarish Patra (recommendation letter). You then take this to the District Administration Office (DAO / CDO Office) in your district to receive the certificate.

**Q: Can I apply for citizenship in a different district from where I was born?**
A: Yes. Apply in your current district of permanent residence. Bring a Migration Certificate (Basha Sarai Praman-Patra) from your previous district's Ward Office. Your current Ward Office issues the Sifarish and your current DAO issues the certificate.

**Q: How long does it take to get a citizenship certificate?**
A: With complete and correct documents, most applicants receive the certificate the same day (within 1–2 hours). If police verification (Sarjaamin) or additional review is needed, it takes 2–5 additional working days. Naturalization cases take 2–4 weeks.

**Q: Do I need to book an appointment at the DAO?**
A: Yes. Online token appointments are required in Kathmandu and most major districts. Book via your DAO's official website, note your token number, and bring the confirmation.

**Q: What is Sanaakhat?**
A: Sanaakhat is the identity verification step at the DAO where at least one parent must be physically present to confirm the applicant's identity and relationship. If both parents are unavailable, a close relative with Nepali citizenship may accompany the applicant.

**Q: Can I apply for citizenship if I was born abroad?**
A: Yes, if at least one of your parents is a Nepali citizen. You apply for citizenship by descent at the DAO in your parents' district. You will need your birth registration from the Nepali embassy or a birth certificate, along with your parents' citizenship documents.

**Q: Is there a fee to apply for a citizenship certificate?**
A: Citizenship by descent and by birth: no fee. Naturalization for a descendant of a Nepali citizen: NPR 100. Naturalization for a foreigner: NPR 500. Additional costs include notary fees (NPR 150–200), photocopying, and ward office charges.

**Q: Can someone apply for citizenship on my behalf?**
A: No. The applicant must be present in person, especially for the Sanaakhat (identity verification) step at the DAO. A parent or close relative must also be present.

**Q: What is the Sifarish Patra and why do I need it?**
A: The Sifarish Patra is a recommendation letter from your Ward Office confirming your birthplace, permanent address, and relationship to your parents. The DAO will not accept any citizenship application without it — it is a mandatory first step.

## Category 2: Documents

**Q: What is the most important document for the citizenship application?**
A: The birth certificate (Janma Darta Praman-Patra) is the primary and mandatory document. All other documents are verified against it.

**Q: What photo size and format is required?**
A: 35mm x 45mm (3.5cm x 4.5cm) passport-size photograph. White background, front-facing, both ears visible, neutral expression, no sunglasses or hat. Prepare at least 4 copies from a professional photo studio.

**Q: My name on my birth certificate is slightly different from my academic certificates. What do I do?**
A: This is the most common rejection reason. Fix the birth record at the Local Registrar first, then apply for citizenship. All name and date of birth entries must match exactly across your birth certificate, academic certificates, and parental citizenship.

**Q: Do I need to bring original documents or just photocopies?**
A: Bring both. Submit photocopies; originals must be brought for verification by the DAO staff. Originals are not taken from you — they are verified and returned.

**Q: What if I don't have an academic certificate (SLC/SEE)?**
A: The academic certificate is used to verify name spelling and date of birth. If unavailable, consult your Ward Office and DAO — alternative verification documents may be accepted depending on the case.

**Q: Is the National Identity Card (NID) required for citizenship?**
A: No — the NID is obtained after citizenship. Your citizenship certificate is the foundational document needed to register for the NID, not the other way around.

## Category 3: Special Situations

**Q: Can I apply for citizenship if my father has passed away?**
A: Yes. Provide your father's death certificate. Your mother can accompany you as the verifying parent. The CDO has authority to accept supporting documents if the father's original citizenship records are unavailable.

**Q: My father's identity is unknown. Can I still get citizenship?**
A: Yes. Under the Nepal Citizenship Second Amendment Act 2082, you can get citizenship by descent through your mother. Both you and your mother must sign a SoGhoshana (Sworn Declaration) before the CDO confirming the father's identity is unknown.

**Q: I am a married woman. Should I keep my father's name or add my husband's name to my citizenship?**
A: You have a choice. Option 1: Keep your maiden citizenship unchanged (no additional steps). Option 2: Update citizenship with husband's name by submitting a marriage registration certificate and Ward Office Sifarish from husband's ward. Option 3: If divorced, revert to maiden name using a divorce certificate and Ward Office recommendation.

**Q: I am a foreign woman married to a Nepali man. How do I get citizenship?**
A: You are eligible for naturalized citizenship. You must have a valid marriage registration certificate and initiate the renunciation of your foreign citizenship. Submit these along with your foreign passport copy and Ward Office Sifarish at the DAO. Processing takes 1–2 weeks.

**Q: I acquired foreign citizenship. Have I lost my Nepali citizenship?**
A: Yes. Under Section 10 of the Nepal Citizenship Act, voluntarily acquiring foreign citizenship results in the automatic loss of Nepali citizenship from that date — no court proceeding is required.

**Q: I previously renounced my Nepali citizenship and now want it back. Is that possible?**
A: Yes. Under Section 11 of the Nepal Citizenship Act, former Nepali citizens who have since renounced their foreign citizenship may re-apply at the DAO with proof of renunciation.

**Q: I was born with dual citizenship. What do I do?**
A: If you hold both Nepali and another country's citizenship at birth, you must formally choose which to retain before age 18 (within 2 years of your 16th birthday). If no formal choice is declared at the DAO, Nepali citizenship lapses automatically.

**Q: What is NRN (Non-Resident Nepali) citizenship and who qualifies?**
A: NRN citizenship is a special legal status for persons of Nepali origin who have acquired citizenship of a non-SAARC country. It grants economic, social, and cultural rights in Nepal but not political or administrative rights. You must prove that your father, mother, grandfather, or grandmother was a Nepali citizen.

**Q: Can an NRN buy property in Nepal?**
A: Yes, with restrictions. NRNs may purchase land for residential purposes: up to 2 Ropani in Kathmandu Valley, 8 Kattha in Terai municipalities, and 10 Ropani in other areas. Prior permission from the Ministry of Foreign Affairs is required.

## Category 4: Lost, Duplicate & Corrections

**Q: I lost my citizenship certificate. What do I do?**
A: File a police report at the nearest police station, get the missing document report, obtain a Ward Office recommendation (Sifarish), and apply for a duplicate (Pratilipi) at the DAO where the original was issued. The duplicate carries the same legal validity as the original.

**Q: Is a duplicate citizenship certificate legally valid?**
A: Yes. A duplicate certificate (Pratilipi) is marked as duplicate but has the same legal validity as the original for all purposes.

**Q: There is an error in my issued citizenship certificate. How do I correct it?**
A: Apply at the same DAO that issued the original certificate with your original citizenship and documents proving the correct information (birth certificate, academic certificates). If the error is the DAO's fault, correction is made without additional fee. If the error is the applicant's fault, standard processing applies.

**Q: Can I make corrections while applying for a duplicate?**
A: Yes. If your name or details have changed since the original (e.g., due to marriage, divorce, or court order), bring supporting documents to incorporate corrections during the duplicate application.

## Category 5: International Use & Translation

**Q: My citizenship certificate is in Nepali. How do I use it abroad?**
A: Engage a registered Notary Public in Nepal to translate and certify the document into English. The fee is approximately NPR 150–200. Always translate and notarize both sides of the certificate.

**Q: What is the process for attestation of the citizenship certificate for use abroad?**
A: The standard chain is: (1) Certified English translation, (2) Notarization by a Nepali Notary Public, (3) Attestation by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MoFA) in Kathmandu, (4) Destination embassy legalization if required. Nepal is not part of the Hague Apostille Convention, so embassy legalization replaces an apostille.

**Q: Can I use my citizenship certificate to verify my identity online?**
A: As of 2026, there is no public online portal for verifying citizenship numbers. Authenticity can only be confirmed through the issuing DAO or via the NID portal (which links to citizenship records). Always use official government sources — moha.gov.np.

## Category 6: Important Rules & Notes

**Q: Can I laminate my citizenship certificate?**
A: No. Laminated certificates cannot be photocopied clearly and are often refused by government offices. Use a protective plastic sleeve instead.

**Q: What rights does a citizenship certificate give me?**
A: It is the foundation for all government rights and services: applying for a passport, opening a bank account, buying property, enrolling in higher education, voting, and accessing all government services.

**Q: What is the difference between a citizenship certificate and a National Identity Card (NID)?**
A: The citizenship certificate (pink paper, issued by the DAO) is the primary proof of Nepali nationality, governed by the Citizenship Act. The NID is a digital smart card issued by the Department of National ID and Civil Registration (DoNIDCR) for day-to-day service delivery. You need citizenship first to get the NID — both documents remain in use simultaneously.

**Q: My application was rejected. Can I appeal?**
A: Yes. Get the written rejection decision from the DAO and file an appeal within 35 days under Section 14(2) of the Nepal Citizenship Act. You may also seek revision within 35 days under Section 18. Do not wait informally after rejection — statutory time limits apply.

**Q: What is Sarjaamin (police verification)?**
A: Sarjaamin is a local inquiry or police verification conducted when the CDO has doubts about the applicant's information, documents, or identity. It adds 2–5 working days to processing time.

**Q: What are the most common reasons for application rejection?**
A: The top reasons are: (1) birth registration mismatch with parental citizenship, (2) filing at the wrong DAO, (3) missing Ward Office Sifarish, (4) parent not present for Sanaakhat, and (5) inconsistent name across documents. Fix records upstream (birth registration) before applying.
