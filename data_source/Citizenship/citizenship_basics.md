## What Is a Citizenship Certificate?

The Nepali Citizenship Certificate is the most important legal document for every Nepali national. It is the foundation for:

- Applying for a passport
- Opening a bank account
- Buying or registering property
- Enrolling in higher education
- Voting in elections
- Accessing all government services

It contains: full name, date of birth, permanent address, photograph, parent's names, gender identity, and a unique citizenship number recognized across all government agencies.

> Nepal citizenship certificate is printed on pink paper and issued exclusively in Nepali (Devanagari script).

## Types of Citizenship in Nepal

### 1. Citizenship by Descent (Vamshaja Nagarikta)
The most common type. Available to anyone whose parent (father or mother) was a Nepali citizen at the time of their birth.

- Available from age 16
- No specific residence period required — parental link is sufficient
- Applies regardless of where the child was born (Nepal or abroad)
- **2082 Amendment:** A child may apply using only the mother's name if the father is unidentified or deceased

### 2. Citizenship by Birth (Janma Siddha Nagarikta)
A historical category only. For persons born inside Nepal before the end of Chaitra 2046 B.S. who have been continuously residing in Nepal.

- **Not available to new applicants** born after 2046 B.S.
- Everyone born after that date must apply through descent or naturalization

### 3. Naturalized Citizenship (Angikrit Nagarikta)
For those who do not qualify by descent or birth. Sub-categories:

| Sub-category | Key Condition |
|---|---|
| Foreign woman married to a Nepali male | Valid marriage certificate + proof of initiating foreign citizenship renunciation |
| Child of Nepali mother and foreign father, residing permanently in Nepal | No foreign citizenship + SoGhoshana (Sworn Declaration) |
| Child born abroad to Nepali mother with unknown father, residing in Nepal | Mother and child both submit SoGhoshana before CDO |
| Special contribution (science, arts, sports) | Minimum 15 years residence + language proficiency + renunciation of foreign citizenship |

### 4. Honorary Citizenship (Sammanaarttha Nagarikta)
Ceremonial recognition by the Government of Nepal for foreign nationals with extraordinary contributions to Nepal.

- No application required — government-initiated
- Does not carry full civil rights (no voting, no property ownership)

## Eligibility — Who Can Apply?

| Citizenship Type | Who Qualifies |
|---|---|
| By Descent | Age 16+ with at least one Nepali citizen parent |
| By Birth | Born in Nepal before Chaitra 2046 B.S. with continuous residence |
| Naturalized – Married | Foreign woman married to Nepali man with valid marriage registration |
| Naturalized – Child of Nepali mother | Child of Nepali mother and foreign father — permanent resident, no foreign citizenship, SoGhoshana |
| Naturalized – Born abroad to Nepali mother | Residing in Nepal + unknown father + SoGhoshana by both mother and child |
| Honorary | Foreign national — government conferred, not applied for |

## Required Documents

### Standard — Citizenship by Descent (Both Parents Nepali)

| Document | Details |
|---|---|
| Birth Certificate (Janma Darta) | Original — primary and mandatory document |
| Parents' Citizenship Certificates | Photocopies — bring originals for verification |
| Ward Office Recommendation (Sifarish Patra) | Confirms birthplace, parental relationship, and permanent address |
| Passport-Size Photos | 3–4 recent photos, white background, both ears visible, 35mm x 45mm |
| Academic Certificate | SLC/SEE or equivalent — to verify name spelling and date of birth |
| Migration Certificate | Only if you permanently moved from another district |
| Marriage Certificate | Required for married female applicants adding husband's name or address |
| Guardian Presence (Sanaakhat) | At least one parent must be physically present at DAO for identity verification |

### When One Parent Is Deceased
- Death Certificate (Mrityu Darta Praman-Patra) of the deceased parent
- If deceased parent's citizenship is unavailable: voter ID, family registration, or local authority testimony may be accepted

### Citizenship Through Mother (Father Unidentified — 2082 Amendment)
- Birth certificate of applicant
- Mother's citizenship certificate
- Ward Office recommendation (Sifarish Patra)
- SoGhoshana (Sworn Declaration) signed by both applicant and mother before the CDO
- 3–4 passport-size photographs

### Photo Requirements
| Detail | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Size | 35mm x 45mm (3.5cm x 4.5cm) |
| Background | Plain white |
| Face | Front-facing, both ears clearly visible |
| Expression | Neutral — no wide smile, no sunglasses or hat |
| Quality | Sharp, in-focus — use a professional photo studio |
| Quantity | Prepare at least 4 copies |

## Application Process — Step by Step

**Step 1 — Visit the Ward Office (Wada Karyalaya)**
- Collect the citizenship application form (Schedule 1 form) — free of charge
- Fill it accurately, cross-checking against your birth certificate and parents' citizenship
- Attach photocopies of all required documents (bring originals)
- Submit to the Ward Office counter
- Ward Office verifies identity, permanent residence, and parental relationship
- Receive the Sifarish Patra (recommendation letter) — typically same day to 1–2 working days

**Step 2 — Book an Appointment at the DAO**
- Book an online token appointment at your DAO's official website
- Online booking is required in Kathmandu and most major districts
- Note your token number and bring the confirmation printout

**Step 3 — Visit the District Administration Office (DAO / CDO Office)**
- Bring all original documents, photocopies, Sifarish Patra, photos, and at least one parent
- Submit your file at the document verification counter

**Step 4 — Identity Verification (Sanaakhat)**
- One parent must be physically present for identity confirmation
- If both parents are unavailable, a close relative with Nepali citizenship may accompany you
- For single-mother cases with unidentified father, the CDO may use police verification

**Step 5 — CDO Signs and Issues the Certificate**
- CDO or authorized officer reviews, signs, and stamps the citizenship certificate
- With complete documents: certificate is issued the same day
- If additional verification (Sarjaamin / police inquiry) is needed: 2–5 additional working days

## Dual Citizenship — Is It Allowed?

**No.** Nepal does not recognize dual citizenship.

- Under Section 10 of the Nepal Citizenship Act, a Nepali citizen who voluntarily acquires foreign nationality automatically loses Nepali citizenship from that date — by law, without any court proceeding
- **Voluntary Renunciation:** Submit original citizenship certificate, passport, photos, and a written application in person at a Nepali embassy or consulate abroad
- **Dual Citizenship at Birth:** If a person holds both Nepali and another country's citizenship at birth, they must choose which to retain before age 18. If no formal choice is made, Nepali citizenship lapses automatically
- **Re-Acquisition:** Former Nepali citizens who later renounce their foreign citizenship may re-apply at the DAO with proof of renunciation (Section 11 of the Citizenship Act)

## Citizenship for Minors — Minor Identity Card (Nabalik Parichaya Patra)

Introduced under the Fourth Amendment Rules 2082 (Rule 16-Ka) for children under 16 who previously had no official government ID.

| Detail | Information |
|---|---|
| Who Can Get It | Children under 16 years of age |
| Documents Required | Birth certificate + at least one parent's citizenship + Ward Office recommendation |
| For Orphanage Children | Institution recommendation + local government Surjaamin (identity verification) |
| Issuing Authority | Ward Office |
| Validity | Until the child turns 16 |
| Fee | No fee (currently) |

> The Minor Identity Card is mandatory for children applying for a passport. Passport applications for minors will not be accepted without verification of this card from the relevant DAO/AAO.

## Online Verification of Citizenship

As of 2026, there is **no public online portal** to verify an arbitrary citizenship number.

- **NID Portal** — citizenship number links to National ID Card enrollment, verifiable through the NID system
- **Voter Check** — verify voter registration status using citizenship number at election.gov.np
- **Direct Inquiry** — contact the issuing DAO in person or via the citizenship certificate verification form on some DAO websites under moha.gov.np
- Beware of third-party websites claiming to offer "Citizenship Number Check" services — use only official government sources

## English Translation of Citizenship Certificate

Citizenship certificates are issued only in Nepali (Devanagari script). For international use (university applications, immigration, overseas employment, foreign banks), a certified English translation is required.

- Engage a registered Notary Public in Nepal
- **Fee:** Approximately NPR 150–200
- Available at Kathmandu (Putali Sadak, Baneswor, New Road) and all 77 district headquarters
- The notarized translation covers both the front and back of the certificate

> **Critical:** Always translate and notarize BOTH sides of the citizenship certificate. A partial translation (front only or back only) will be rejected for official international purposes.

**For use abroad (embassies, immigration):** The standard accepted chain is:
1. Certified English translation by a recognized translator
2. Notarization by a Nepali Notary Public
3. Attestation by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MoFA), Kathmandu
4. Destination embassy legalization where required

> Nepal is not a party to the Hague Apostille Convention, so embassy legalization replaces an apostille.

## Lost or Duplicate Citizenship Certificate

1. File a police report at the nearest police station — get a copy of the missing document report
2. Obtain Ward Office recommendation — submit police report and request a Sifarish letter for duplicate citizenship
3. Apply at the DAO where the original was issued (or your current permanent address DAO)
   - Submit: police report, Ward Office Sifarish, any photocopy of old citizenship, photos, formal application
4. Receive duplicate certificate — marked as "Duplicate" (Pratilipi) but carries the same legal validity as the original

> If your name or details have changed (e.g., due to marriage, divorce, or court order), bring supporting documents to incorporate corrections during the duplicate application.

## Appeal Process

If your citizenship application is refused or cancelled:

- Get the written decision from the DAO
- You may appeal within **35 days** under Section 14(2) of the Nepal Citizenship Act
- Revision can also be sought within **35 days** under Section 18 of the Act
- Do not wait informally after rejection — statutory time limits apply

---

## Important Notes

- The citizenship certificate is the foundational document — it is required before obtaining a National ID Card (NID), passport, bank account, property registration, or voter registration
- **Do not laminate** the citizenship certificate — laminated certificates cannot be photocopied clearly and may be refused by government offices; use a protective plastic sleeve instead
- All details on the certificate must exactly match your birth certificate and parents' citizenship — mismatches are the most common reason for rejection
- You must apply at the DAO covering your permanent address as recorded on your parental citizenship — not where you currently live or rent
- The Ward Office Sifarish step cannot be skipped — the DAO will not accept any file without it
