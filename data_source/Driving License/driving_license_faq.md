# Driving License FAQ — Nepal

---

## Category 1: Application & Eligibility

**Q: What is the minimum age to apply for a driving license in Nepal?**
A: 16 years for Category A (motorcycle, scooter, moped). 18 years for Category B (car, jeep) and all other categories including commercial and heavy vehicles.

**Q: Where do I apply for a driving license?**
A: Apply online at applydl.dotm.gov.np or your province's portal. You then visit any DoTM Transport Management Office in Nepal — you are not restricted to your home district.

**Q: When is the online driving license form available?**
A: Sunday to Wednesday only, between 6:00 AM and 10:00 PM (Nepal Standard Time). Not available on public holidays or outside these hours.

**Q: Can I apply from my mobile phone at home?**
A: Yes. The full online application is completed at applydl.dotm.gov.np from any mobile phone or computer. You only need to visit the transport office for biometrics, medical test, payment, and exams.

**Q: Can I apply at any transport office, or only in my home district?**
A: You can apply at any DoTM transport office across Nepal. You are not restricted to your home district or province.

**Q: Can I apply for both Category A and Category B at the same time?**
A: Yes. Many applicants apply for both simultaneously to complete both written and trial exams in fewer visits. Separate government fees apply for each category.

**Q: What document do I need to start the application?**
A: Your Nepali Citizenship Certificate (Nagarikta) — front and back — and a mobile number registered in your name. That is sufficient to begin.

**Q: How do I auto-fill the form?**
A: Scan the QR code in your National ID or the Nagarik App to auto-fill your details instantly, removing the need for manual data entry.

**Q: What happens if I see "Details not found from Citizenship Information Management System"?**
A: This is a known government system error. If your details are entered correctly from your citizenship certificate, simply click Next and continue — your application will be accepted.

## Category 2: Fees & Payment

**Q: What is the total cost of getting a driving license in Nepal?**
A: For Category A (motorcycle): approximately NPR 2,400–2,700 total. For Category B (car): approximately NPR 3,100–3,500 total. This includes: application fee (NPR 500), medical test, trial token, government fee after trial (NPR 1,500 for A, NPR 2,000 for B), and smart card (NPR 200).

**Q: When do I pay the government fee?**
A: On biometric day at the transport office counter, after medical clearance. You have up to 18 months from biometric day to pay if you cannot pay immediately. Keep all receipts.

**Q: Is the online application free?**
A: Yes — online form submission is free of charge.

**Q: How much does a retrial cost?**
A: NPR 500 per retrial attempt for the practical trial exam.

**Q: How much does a duplicate license cost?**
A: NPR 500 government fee for a duplicate/reissued license.

## Category 3: Exams

**Q: What does the written exam (Likhit Pariksha) cover?**
A: Road signs, driving rules, fines, and traffic safety. Free practice questions are available at unionnepal.com/driving-license-exam-preparation.

**Q: Is the written exam required for renewals?**
A: No. The written exam is required only for new license applications. It is waived for renewals, adding a vehicle category, and duplicate licenses.

**Q: Is the trial exam required for renewals?**
A: No. Like the written exam, the trial exam is required only for new license applications. Not required for renewals, category additions, or duplicates.

**Q: Do I need to retake the written exam when adding a new vehicle category?**
A: In most cases, no. If you hold Category B and are adding Category A (or vice versa), the written exam is typically waived. Only the practical trial exam for the new category is required.

**Q: What happens if I fail the trial exam?**
A: You can rebook a retrial. Pay NPR 500 per attempt at the same transport office. Maximum 3 attempts within 18 months. After 3 failures, wait 3 months then restart the full process.

**Q: How many times can I retake the trial exam?**
A: Maximum 3 times within 18 months from your biometric day. After 3 failures, you must wait 3 months and then restart the entire application process from the beginning.

**Q: When is the trial exam result announced?**
A: Immediately after the test — the examiner marks the result on the spot.

## Category 4: Biometric & Medical

**Q: Is biometric registration required every time I visit?**
A: No. Biometric registration and the medical test are required only for new applications and renewals. Adding a vehicle category or applying for a duplicate license does not require repeating biometrics.

**Q: What happens during the medical exam?**
A: Two tests: (1) Long Distance Vision Test — identify the direction of the letter "E" at 20 metres, covering each eye separately. (2) Colour Vision Test — identify numbers in a colour-coded book. Prescription glasses are accepted. If you fail the in-office test, an external eye clinic certificate is generally accepted.

**Q: Do I need to bring a photo for the application?**
A: No. Your photograph is captured live at the transport office during biometric registration. No separate photo is required.

## Category 5: Smart Card & Status

**Q: How long does it take to receive the smart card?**
A: Standard processing varies — from a few weeks to a year depending on the district and printing backlog. SMS notification is sent when the card is ready. For urgent needs, apply for the Emergency/Fast-Print service (card ready in 3 working days).

**Q: How do I check if my smart card is printed?**
A: Visit dotm.gov.np, go to "Smart Card License Details Search," enter your license number and date of birth. No login required. You can also use the Merokalam or Union Nepal print check tools.

**Q: Can I drive while waiting for my smart card?**
A: Yes. Until the smart card is issued, you may receive a temporary driving slip (chalani purja) which legally allows you to drive within Nepal.

**Q: How do I verify if a driving license is genuine?**
A: Visit dotm.gov.np, go to "Smart Card License Details Search," enter the license holder's date of birth and license number. If correct details appear, the license is genuine. If no details appear, visit the nearest transport office immediately.

## Category 6: Renewal, Duplicate & Special Cases

**Q: When should I renew my driving license?**
A: In the final year of validity. You have a 35-day grace period after expiry with no penalty. After the grace period, NPR 200 per year of delay is added to the renewal fee.

**Q: Can I drive on an expired license during the 35-day grace period?**
A: Technically no — driving on an expired license is illegal under Nepal traffic law, even within the grace period. Renew before the expiry date.

**Q: I lost my driving license. What should I do?**
A: File a police report, then apply online at applydl.dotm.gov.np for a duplicate license. Pay NPR 500. No written exam, trial exam, or biometric test required.

**Q: Can I change my transport office after submitting the form?**
A: No. Once submitted, the transport office cannot be changed. Choose carefully during the form-filling process.

**Q: How do I correct an error in my submitted form?**
A: Visit the transport office before your appointment date. The officer will cancel your existing form entry and create a corrected one. A correction fee of approximately NPR 400–500 applies and a new appointment date is issued.

**Q: Is my Nepal driving license valid abroad?**
A: For tourism and short-term travel, it is generally recognized in many countries — especially with an International Driving Permit (IDP). For long-term stays or employment, most countries require you to convert your license or obtain a local license. Check destination country rules.

**Q: What is an International Driving Permit (IDP) and how do I get one?**
A: An IDP is a multilingual document that enables you to drive in up to 190 countries using your Nepal license. Apply at the DoTM office. Always carry both IDP and original Nepal driving license. Available in 1, 2, or 3-year validity.

**Q: Can someone else apply for my emergency fast-print license?**
A: No. The fast-print application must be submitted directly by the license holder from their own registered mobile account. Third-party submission is not permitted.

**Q: My license is expired and damaged. What should I do?**
A: Complete the renewal process first. DoTM will issue a new smart card as part of the renewal — you do not need a separate duplicate application.

## Category 7: Technical & Portal Issues

**Q: Is the new province-wise portal system fully stable?**
A: The national portal at applydl.dotm.gov.np is active and reliable. The newer province-wise portals with QR auto-fill are a recent rollout and may have occasional issues. Use the national portal if experiencing problems with a provincial one.

**Q: Are new applications and renewals fully online now?**
A: Yes. As per the DoTM notice effective 2083/04/01 B.S., new applications, renewals, category additions, and duplicate licenses — including government fee payment — are now completed fully online.
