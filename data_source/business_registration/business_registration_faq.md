# Business Registration FAQ — Nepal

---

## Category 1: Basics & Eligibility

**Q: What is company registration in Nepal?**
A: Company registration is the legal process of incorporating a business entity with the Office of Company Registrar (OCR) under the Companies Act 2063. Once registered, the company becomes a separate legal person — it can own property, enter contracts, open bank accounts, and protect shareholders from personal liability.

**Q: Who can register a company in Nepal?**
A: Any Nepali citizen aged 18 or above can register a company. A single person can form a Private Limited Company (minimum 1 shareholder, 1 director). Foreign nationals can also register with prior approval from the Department of Industry (DOI).

**Q: Where do I register a company in Nepal?**
A: Online through the CAMIS portal at camis.ocr.gov.np, managed by the Office of Company Registrar (OCR) under the Ministry of Industry, Commerce and Supplies.

**Q: What is the most common type of company to register?**
A: A Private Limited Company (Pvt. Ltd.). It requires minimum 1 shareholder, has maximum 101 shareholders, offers limited liability, and is suitable for startups, SMEs, consulting firms, IT businesses, and trading companies.

**Q: Can one person register a company alone?**
A: Yes. Under the Companies Act 2063, one person can form a Private Limited Company as a single shareholder and sole director simultaneously.

**Q: Do I need to visit the OCR office in person?**
A: Most of the process is done online through CAMIS. However, some district offices may require physical submission of original documents for verification. Check the current requirements on camis.ocr.gov.np before starting.

**Q: How long does company registration take?**
A: With complete and correct documents: 7–10 working days. If OCR raises queries: up to 21 working days. Name reservation alone takes 1–3 working days. PAN registration at IRD adds 1–2 more days.

**Q: Is there a fast-track / urgent registration service?**
A: Yes. OCR offers priority processing with a 50% additional surcharge on the government fee. Some law firms report completing simple Private Limited registrations in 2–3 business days with complete documentation and professional assistance.

## Category 2: Company Name

**Q: How do I check if a company name is available?**
A: Log in to camis.ocr.gov.np and search the proposed company name in both English and Nepali. You can also search registered company details at ocr.gov.np.

**Q: Can I submit the name in only one language?**
A: No. The name must be submitted in both English and Nepali on the CAMIS portal.

**Q: What are the rules for choosing a company name?**
A: The name must be unique, not similar to any existing registered company, and must not contain restricted words (Nepal, Bank, University, Government, National, Royal, Insurance) without separate approval. It cannot be too generic or misleading about the company's activities.

**Q: My proposed name was rejected. What do I do?**
A: Prepare 2–3 alternative names before submitting. Make names more specific and distinctive. Resubmit through CAMIS — no additional fee for re-submission. Approval takes 1–3 working days per attempt.

**Q: How long after name approval do I have to complete registration?**
A: 90 days from name reservation approval. If you do not complete registration within this period, the name reservation expires and you must reserve again.

## Category 3: Documents

**Q: What are the main documents required for company registration?**
A: Application form (Anusuchi 1 with NPR 5 revenue stamp), Memorandum of Association (MOA / Prabandha Patra) — 2 signed copies, Articles of Association (AOA / Niyamawali) — 2 signed copies, citizenship certificates of all promoters, registered office proof (rental agreement or ownership document), passport-size photos of promoters, and director consent letters.

**Q: What are MOA and AOA?**
A: MOA (Memorandum of Association / Prabandha Patra) defines the company's name, registered office, business objectives, authorized capital, and liability type. AOA (Articles of Association / Niyamawali) defines internal governance rules — board meetings, shareholder rights, voting, dividends. Both are signed and submitted in two copies each.

**Q: Do I need a lawyer to draft MOA and AOA?**
A: Not legally required, but strongly recommended. Formatting errors in these documents are the most common cause of OCR queries and delays. Many founders use a legal consultant or company secretary to draft these documents.

**Q: Can I use my home address as the registered office?**
A: Yes, if you have the landlord's (or parent's) written consent. A physical address is mandatory — a PO Box is not accepted. You need a rental agreement or ownership document as proof.

**Q: What document format does OCR accept for uploads?**
A: PDF and JPEG formats. Files must be clearly legible — blurry or cut-off scans are frequently rejected.

## Category 4: Fees & Payment

**Q: How much does company registration cost?**
A: The government fee starts at NPR 1,000 for authorized capital up to NPR 1 Lakh. A realistic total budget for a standard Private Limited Company (including government fees, MOA/AOA drafting, notarization, ward office registration, and miscellaneous) is NPR 15,000–25,000.

**Q: How is the government fee calculated?**
A: Based on the company's authorized capital. Higher authorized capital means higher government fee. The fee ranges from NPR 1,000 (up to 1 Lakh capital) to NPR 43,500+ (up to NPR 10 Crore capital), with NPR 4,000 per additional NPR 10 Lakh above that.

**Q: How do I pay the registration fee?**
A: Through the CAMIS payment gateway via e-banking, mobile banking (eSewa, Khalti), or debit/credit card. Fees above NPR 5,000 require a bank deposit voucher uploaded to the portal.

**Q: Is PAN registration free?**
A: Yes. PAN registration at the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) is free of charge.

**Q: Is VAT registration free?**
A: Yes. VAT registration at the IRD is also free.

## Category 5: Post-Registration

**Q: What do I do after receiving the Certificate of Incorporation?**
A: Several mandatory steps follow — PAN registration at IRD (within 7 days), ward office business registration (within 30 days), first board meeting (within 30 days), Share Lagat filing at OCR (within 90 days), open a company bank account, appoint an auditor before the first AGM, and file annual returns within 60 days of fiscal year end.

**Q: Is PAN registration mandatory after company registration?**
A: Yes. PAN is mandatory before any financial transaction — you cannot open a bank account, raise an invoice, hire employees on payroll, or file taxes without a PAN. Register at IRD within 7 days of company registration.

**Q: Is VAT registration mandatory?**
A: Not for all companies. VAT is mandatory if annual turnover exceeds NPR 50 Lakhs (for goods/trading) or NPR 20 Lakhs (for services). Voluntary VAT registration is allowed even below the threshold to claim VAT refunds on expenses.

**Q: What is Share Lagat and why is it important?**
A: Share Lagat is the filing of shareholder details with the OCR. It must be filed within 90 days of company registration. Late filing results in compounding financial penalties.

**Q: When must the first board meeting be held?**
A: Within 30 days of incorporation.

**Q: When must the Annual General Meeting (AGM) be held?**
A: Within 6 months of the fiscal year end.

**Q: When must annual returns be filed with OCR?**
A: Within 60 days of fiscal year end. Late filing results in penalties.

**Q: Do I need to appoint an auditor?**
A: Yes. An auditor must be appointed before the first AGM. Notify the OCR of the appointment within 15 days.

## Category 6: Foreign Investment & Special Cases

**Q: Can a foreign national register a company in Nepal?**
A: Yes. Foreign nationals can register companies in Nepal, but must first obtain foreign investment approval from the Department of Industry (DOI) or Investment Board Nepal (IBN). Minimum investment per foreign investor: NPR 2 Crore (20 Million).

**Q: What is the minimum FDI amount in Nepal?**
A: NPR 2,00,00,000 (2 Crore / 20 Million) per foreign investor.

**Q: Can an NRN (Non-Resident Nepali) register a company without being in Nepal?**
A: Yes. NRNs can register remotely through a Power of Attorney (PoA) executed in favor of a local representative. The PoA must be notarized in the country of residence and apostilled (or embassy-attested). The representative completes the entire CAMIS process in Nepal.

**Q: What is a Foreign Branch Office vs a Foreign Liaison Office?**
A: A Branch Office can conduct commercial activities in Nepal (generate revenue, sign contracts). A Liaison Office can only conduct non-commercial activities (research, coordination, market study) — no revenue generation allowed. Both must register with OCR.

**Q: My business is in a regulated sector. Do I register at OCR first or get a sector license first?**
A: Get the sector-specific license or approval first, then register at OCR. Operating without the required sectoral license is illegal even if you have an OCR registration certificate.

## Category 7: Amendments, Compliance & Closure

**Q: How do I change a director after registration?**
A: Notify the OCR through CAMIS within 15 days of any director appointment or resignation. A board resolution authorizing the change is required.

**Q: Can I change my company name after registration?**
A: Yes. Apply for a new name reservation through CAMIS, then file a name change amendment once the new name is approved. Standard name reservation rules apply.

**Q: How do I increase the authorized capital of my company?**
A: File a capital increase amendment through CAMIS with supporting shareholder resolution and pay the additional government fee. This must be done before issuing new shares.

**Q: I want to close my company. What do I do?**
A: Apply for voluntary dissolution or strike-off through CAMIS under the Directive Relating to Special Deregistration 2025. Clear all pending annual returns, obtain a No Objection Letter from IRD confirming all taxes are paid, then submit the dissolution application. Do not simply stop operating — penalties for non-compliance compound over time.

**Q: What happens if I miss the annual return filing deadline?**
A: Penalties apply under the Companies Act. These compound over time. File as soon as possible and pay any applicable late filing fees.

**Q: How do I verify that another company is genuinely registered?**
A: Visit ocr.gov.np and search by company name or registration number. The search returns registration status, registered office, directors, and other public details. Always verify before entering a business partnership or investment.

## Category 8: Practical Tips

**Q: Should I use a legal consultant for company registration?**
A: For straightforward Private Limited companies with Nepali shareholders, the CAMIS process is manageable without a consultant. However, for foreign investment structures, regulated sectors, or complex capital arrangements, a Nepal Bar Council-registered corporate lawyer or company secretary saves time and prevents costly errors.

**Q: What is the most common reason for delays in company registration?**
A: Document rejection due to formatting errors — especially in MOA and AOA. Mismatched company names between the OCR-approved name and the MOA are the single most common cause of rejection. Always copy the approved name exactly.

**Q: How do I check the status of my registration application?**
A: Log in to your CAMIS account at camis.ocr.gov.np. Your application status, any query notices, and payment status are all visible on your dashboard.

**Q: Can I register a company in a province outside Kathmandu?**
A: Yes. OCR has provincial offices across Nepal. You do not need to come to Kathmandu. Use the provincial office in your area and the corresponding CAMIS portal.
